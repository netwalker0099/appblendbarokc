The Blend Bar

Perfume stand intake & billing platform — executive briefing

f8550fc · 2026-07-27 · INTERNAL
01Executive summary

Where this stands

The Blend Bar app records who bought which perfume blend and takes their payment through Square. The staff app, customer portal and public website are live. Payment handling was rebuilt this cycle: Square replaced Squarespace, an online buy path was added to shared scent links, and a fourth bottle size was introduced.

Every part of the payment system works against a built-in simulator. None of it has ever contacted Square. No credentials exist on the server, so no request has ever been sent, and the system cannot take money today.

The decision is whether to continue. The remaining work to reach first revenue is small and well defined: obtain Square credentials, validate against Square's free sandbox, switch on. The larger open question is operational rather than technical — nobody is assigned to fulfil an online order.

02How it works

Three paths through the system

At the bar

Staff record the customer and the blend they made. The order is logged but nothing is owed yet. Staff then build a cart, and the customer scans a QR code and pays on their own phone.

From a shared link

A customer shares a scent link. Whoever receives it picks a size, enters an email, and pays. The blend is then made by hand — it does not exist at the moment of purchase.

Checking the books

A report compares what the app recorded selling against what Square actually collected, and sorts every difference into a named bucket with an action attached.

Card details are entered on Square's own payment page and never reach this system. That is a deliberate compliance choice: it keeps the business at the lightest tier of card-industry obligation (PCI SAQ-A) and means a breach of this server cannot expose card numbers, because they were never stored here.

04Platform fit

Where this sits

What it replaced

Squarespace was previously the downstream system. It never collected money — staff marked orders paid by hand, which meant the records depended on someone remembering. That connection has been removed entirely.

What it defers to

Square owns payments, card data and receipts. This system owns customers, scent formulas, blends and orders. The two are joined by a shared reference on every sale, which is what makes the reconciliation report possible.

Where it runs

One self-hosted server running three components (database, application, web server), with certificates renewed automatically. No third-party hosting dependency beyond Square itself.

What it does not do

It sends no email — Square sends the payment receipt. It has no shipping or fulfilment workflow. Custom blends cannot be shared or bought online; only set scents can.

05Risks

What could go wrong

The live payment connection has never been tested

The integration follows Square's published specification, but no request has ever left this server. Expect small corrections during first setup. Mitigation: Square provides a free sandbox with test cards. A written step-by-step validation sequence already exists. Do not switch straight to live payments.

Nobody owns online order fulfilment

A blend bought through a shared link does not exist when it is paid for. The order is flagged for staff and the buyer is told it is made by hand, but no process routes it to anyone. Mitigation: none built. This is an operational decision, not a software gap.

Customer data sits on one server with manual backups

All customer records, preferences and order history live on a single host. A backup exists as a button an administrator can press. Mitigation: automated, scheduled, off-site backups are specified but not built. Until then, loss of the host means loss of everything since the last manual download.

The booking terms are website copy, not a signed agreement

Deposit and cancellation terms are published where guests read them before enquiring, which is the right place. But a website section is not something a client actively agrees to. Mitigation: put the same wording on the invoice or an event agreement. This is a legal question, not a software one.

Spray cannot be sold until it is priced

The size exists throughout the system but carries no price, so it is hidden from customers. Mitigation: ten minutes in the admin screen. Deliberate — the system will not invent a price.

06Recommendation

Continue, with a short proving sequence

The build is further along than the "cannot take money" headline suggests. What is missing is not months of engineering — it is an account credential and one working session. The case for stopping would have to rest on the business no longer wanting online payments, not on the state of the code.

Sequence

  1. Create a Square sandbox application; hand over the token and location identifier.
  2. Connect it and run a full test purchase with a Square test card.
  3. Confirm the reconciliation report matches the Square dashboard exactly.
  4. Switch on automatic payment notifications.
  5. Swap in production credentials and take one small real payment as a final check.
  6. Set Spray prices and open the size for sale.

Steps 1–5 are a single short session once credentials exist.

Decision asks

  • Continue or stop. The explicit go/no-go on finishing this work.
  • Square account access. Sandbox credentials first, production after validation. Nothing else blocks revenue.
  • An owner for online orders. A named person who watches for them and makes the blend.
  • Spray pricing. Per scent, and for bespoke blends.
  • Legal review of the booking terms, if deposits are being collected.
  • A decision on automated backups — accept the current manual process, or fund the automated one.

Bottom line

The payment system is built and tested, but has never once spoken to Square — so nothing about it is proven where money is concerned. Closing that gap needs an account credential and one working session, not more engineering. The real unresolved question is who makes and ships a blend somebody buys online at midnight.
